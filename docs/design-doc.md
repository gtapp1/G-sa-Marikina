## Problem Statement

Small food businesses in Marikina City (home-based bakers, starting restos, street food vendors) make great products but can't reach customers. Their current marketing is scattered across Facebook walls, FB groups, campus pages, tarpaulin signs, and hoping food vloggers notice them. There is no single, persistent, Marikina-specific platform where:
- A business owner can have a shareable page that accumulates reviews and trust over time
- A consumer can browse, discover, and review local food businesses in one place

The gap: Marikeño (marikeno.com) writes about Marikina food but doesn't let owners self-list. GrabFood/FoodPanda take 30% commission. Facebook groups are noisy and posts get buried in hours. No one owns their presence on Facebook.

## Demand Evidence

- Founder's friend sells cookies, posts across multiple FB groups and campus pages. Called the platform idea "revolutionary for small biz owners." Suggested curated features for starting businesses.
- Observation: business owners post the same product photos across 5+ groups daily — repetitive work with diminishing returns.
- No existing self-service food business directory exists specifically for Marikina.
- YUMYUMPO validated the hyperlocal model in El Nido, Philippines (free listings, local discovery). Same country, different city. Proof the model works in PH context.

## Status Quo

Current workarounds for Marikina food business owners:
1. **Facebook wall posts** — limited to existing friends, no discovery by strangers
2. **Facebook group posts** — get buried in hours, mixed with unrelated content, no persistent presence
3. **Campus/community page posts** — scatter-shot, time-consuming to maintain across multiple pages
4. **Tarpaulin signs** — zero online reach, limited to foot traffic on their street
5. **Word of mouth** — works but doesn't scale
6. **Praying a food vlogger visits** — no control, purely luck-based

Cost: hours of daily posting across groups for minimal incremental reach. No accumulating trust (each post starts from zero). No single link to share that represents the business.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

**Primary business user:** Home-based food sellers and starting restos in Marikina. Specifically: the founder's friend who sells cookies and currently posts across 5+ FB groups daily. She wants one link she can share that showcases her products with social proof.

**Primary consumer user:** Marikina residents (and nearby areas) who want to discover local food. Currently they scroll Facebook groups or rely on vlogger recommendations.

**Narrowest wedge:** A shareable business page with photos, menu/products, location, and customer reviews. Business owner gets a link (e.g., gsamarikina.com/sweet-cookies-ni-friend). That link is the product. Everything else builds on top.

## Constraints

- Solo developer (founder building it alone)
- No time pressure — willing to invest effort to make it right
- Must be responsive/adaptive across all screen sizes (most Filipino users access via phone)
- Must be simple enough for non-tech-savvy business owners to create a listing
- Needs to solve chicken-and-egg problem (empty platform = dead platform)
- Geographic scope: Marikina City and nearby areas (Antipolo, Cainta, San Mateo, Montalban)

## Premises

1. Small food businesses in Marikina struggle with discovery, not product quality.
2. The current workaround (posting across multiple FB groups) is painful enough that business owners would use a dedicated platform for extra reach.
3. Consumers in Marikina would use a local food discovery tool if it had enough listings and social proof.
4. "Marikina-specific" is a feature — hyperlocal focus creates community identity and trust.
5. Reviews and comments are the core value driver — social proof turns a directory into a discovery engine.
6. Content-first approach (founder curates initial listings) solves the chicken-and-egg problem.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: "Shareable Storefront" (Minimal Viable)
Each business gets a single page (like Linktree for food businesses). Name, photos, menu/products, location, contact, reviews. Ships in 2-4 weeks.
- **Pros:** Ships fast, instant value for business owners (a shareable link), low complexity.
- **Cons:** No discovery engine, consumers only find businesses via direct link, no network effect.

### Approach B: "Local Yelp for Marikina" (Ideal Architecture)
Full discovery platform with search, filters, map view, trending feed, curated sections, consumer accounts.
- **Pros:** Consumers have a reason to browse, network effects kick in, differentiated from FB groups.
- **Cons:** Chicken-and-egg risk, longer build time, needs both sides to show up.

### Approach C: "Curated Food Map" (Content-First)
Start as a curated map of Marikina food spots the founder personally recommends. Build audience first, open self-service later.
- **Pros:** Solves chicken-and-egg, builds audience before platform, feels personal.
- **Cons:** Founder is bottleneck, doesn't immediately help business owners self-list.

## Recommended Approach

**Combined B+C: Content-First Platform**

Build the full platform architecture (Approach B) but launch with curated content (Approach C). Three phases:

### Phase 1: Curated Launch (Weeks 1-4)
- Founder personally adds 20-30 Marikina food business listings as MDX/JSON data files (no database, no backend)
- Static Next.js site deployed on Vercel
- Interactive map with MapLibre GL JS + OpenStreetMap (smooth fly-to animations, marker clustering)
- Category browsing (cookies, milk tea, street food, home-cooked meals, restos, etc.)
- Each listing page: name, photos, products/menu, map pin, contact/order info, founder's review
- Responsive/adaptive design (works on all screen sizes)
- "G sa Marikina" branding with personal curation voice
- JSON-LD structured data + OG tags on every listing page
- **Scope boundary:** No user accounts, no reviews from others, no backend API. This is a deployed static site with a map. Shippable in 2-3 weekends.

### Phase 2: Open Platform (Weeks 5-12)
- Migrate listing data from static JSON/MDX to Supabase PostgreSQL
- Business owner registration and self-service listing creation (email magic link auth)
- "Claim your business" flow (email verification to contact email on file)
- Consumer accounts for leaving reviews and ratings
- Star ratings (1-5) + text reviews with optional photo uploads
- Basic search and filter (by category, by barangay, by rating)
- "New on G sa Marikina" feed
- "Report review" button with manual founder moderation

### Phase 3: Growth Features (Weeks 13+)
- "Trending in Marikina" feed (algorithm: recent reviews x2 + page views x1, daily decay)
- Curated collections: "Starting Biz Owner Spotlight," "Best of [Category]," "Hidden Gems"
- "Near Me" location-based discovery (with graceful fallback if permission denied)
- Business owner dashboard (view count, review notifications)
- "Copy Link" share button (achieves 80% of sharing goal with minimal effort)
- Future consideration: promoted listings as monetization (only after product-market fit confirmed)

### Technical Architecture

**Committed stack:**
- **Frontend:** Next.js 14+ (App Router, SSR for SEO)
- **Styling:** Tailwind CSS + shadcn/ui (pre-built accessible components: cards, forms, modals, buttons)
- **Backend:** Next.js API routes / Server Actions (no separate backend needed for Phase 1-2)
- **Database:** PostgreSQL via Supabase (free tier: 500MB, includes auth, realtime)
- **ORM:** Drizzle ORM (type-safe database queries, catches bugs at compile time)
- **Validation:** Zod (schema validation for forms, API inputs, and database inserts. Define once, validate everywhere.)
- **Maps:** MapLibre GL JS + OpenStreetMap tiles (zero cost, WebGL-rendered, smooth animations, handles marker clustering natively, used via react-map-gl)
- **Auth:** Clerk (free tier: 10,000 MAUs, pre-built sign-in/sign-up components, social logins built-in, excellent Next.js App Router integration. Phase 1: no auth needed. Phase 2: drop in Clerk components for business owner and consumer accounts.)
- **Email:** Resend (free tier: 3,000 emails/month for magic links + review notifications)
- **Hosting:** Vercel (free tier, auto-deploy on git push)
- **Image storage:** Cloudinary (free tier: 25GB bandwidth/month, sufficient for Phase 1-2. Migrate to Cloudflare R2 if exceeding 5,000 monthly users.)
- **SEO:** next-seo + JSON-LD LocalBusiness schema on each listing, dynamic OG meta tags, auto-generated sitemap.xml
- **Analytics:** Vercel Analytics (free tier, tracks page views and top listings for trending algorithm)

**Phase 1 simplification:** The 20-30 curated listings can be stored as MDX files or a simple JSON data file. No database needed until Phase 2 (user accounts, reviews). This means Phase 1 is effectively a static Next.js site with a map. Drastically simpler to build and deploy.

### Key Design Decisions
- **Responsive/adaptive:** Works on phone, tablet, and desktop. 90%+ of target users will access via mobile.
- **Language:** UI chrome in English. User-generated content (reviews, business descriptions) will naturally be Taglish. No i18n framework needed for Phase 1.
- **No delivery:** Not competing with GrabFood. This is discovery and reviews, not logistics.
- **Free for business owners:** Never charge for basic listing. Monetization (promoted listings) is a future consideration after product-market fit, not a phase milestone.
- **SEO-optimized:** Each listing uses JSON-LD LocalBusiness schema, dynamic OG tags for social sharing, and auto-generated sitemap.xml
- **Offline fallback:** If user has poor connectivity, the listing pages should still load (static generation helps). Map tiles may not load — show a text-based address as fallback.
- **Moderation plan:** Phase 1 (founder-curated) = no moderation needed. Phase 2 = founder manually reviews flagged reviews. Simple "Report" button on reviews. No automated moderation until traffic warrants it.
- **Business claim flow:** Business owners claim their listing via email verification to the contact email on file. If disputed, founder resolves manually.
- **Location permissions:** If user denies location access for "Near Me," default to showing all Marikina listings sorted by rating. No blocking UX.
- **Trending algorithm (Phase 3):** Ranked by (reviews in last 7 days x 2) + (page views in last 7 days x 1), decayed daily. Simple, transparent.

### Accessibility
```
- Keyboard: all interactive elements reachable via Tab key
- Focus indicator: 2px --primary orange outline on :focus-visible
- Images: descriptive alt text ("[Business] - [dish name]")
- Map pins: aria-label "Location of [Business] in [barangay]"
- Ratings: aria-label "Rated [N] out of 5 stars"
- Category pills: role="tablist", aria-selected on active
- Contrast: body text on cream = 10.5:1 ✓, caption on cream = 4.7:1 ✓
- Orange text: use --primary-text (#C2410C, 5.2:1) for small text
- Touch targets: 44px minimum on all interactive elements
- Reduced motion: respect prefers-reduced-motion (no fly-to, no transitions)
```

### AI Slop Prevention Rules
```
- NO 3-column feature grids with icons in colored circles
- NO generic hero text ("Welcome to..." / "Your all-in-one...")
- NO decorative blobs, floating circles, or wavy SVG dividers
- NO uniform border-radius on every element
- NO centered-everything layout
- Food photos ARE the visual design — they provide the color and life
- Listing cards: photo on top, info below. Not cards-with-left-border.
- Each category uses its actual emoji, not a generic icon-in-circle
- The homepage hero is a REAL photo of Marikina food, not an illustration
- Map uses warm-toned custom tile style, not default gray OSM
```
